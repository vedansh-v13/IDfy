Product Design Case Study

Verification that builds trust without breaking the rental funnel.

This platform lets people rent high-value items from each other: cameras, drones, designer clothing. Both parties verify before any transaction happens. The tension is simple: verification protects the platform, but asking for personal details too early pushes away users who haven't seen the value yet.

The Business Problem

People won't hand over expensive gear to strangers without verification. But forcing every new sign-up through an ID check destroys conversion rates. The business needs a flow that meets security standards without creating a bottleneck at the front door.

The User Problem

New users want to browse first, not upload passports. When they do decide to rent or list, they need to know why their data is required, how long the check takes, and what happens if the scan fails. Unanswered, those questions cause people to drop out from frustration or suspicion.

The Decision

Verification is triggered by intent, not by account creation.

Instead of blocking users at sign-up, we tie verification to actions that carry actual financial risk: sending a rental request or publishing a listing. Browsing stays open to everyone. Crucially, these verification tiers act as the foundational inputs for a user's transaction Trust Score, converting completed identity and security checks directly into public credibility indicators.

Friction at the moment of purchase is the deliberate trade-off.

An upfront ID check satisfies security early but asks users to do real work before they know whether they want the platform at all. Tiering that check delays it until it is actually necessary.

Casual visitors browse freely. Friction only appears when a user's actions create financial risk. The downside is real: that friction now lands at the exact moment when intent is highest, right when someone clicks "Rent" or "List Item." Confusion or delay at that point is costly. The design doesn't paper over this tension. It accepts it, and bets that clear UI can carry users through the check once they reach it.

Identity verification confirms who a user is. Completed verification tiers contribute directly to a user's Trust Score. Trust grows further through observable marketplace history, including completed rental numbers and response speed. The system recognizes exceptional behavior through earned Trust Badges, awarded only when metrics cross high performance thresholds. This Trust Score remains fully explainable and open, allowing users to inspect the contributing factors on any listing page.

The Experience

Four screens, built for specific decisions.

Each screen in this prototype answers a specific product decision. None of them are padding.

Marketplace Home

Tier 0 in practice: unrestricted access, no sign-in required. Trust is built through specific facts, rental history and response times, not generic rating badges.

Item Details

The decision point. Verification is introduced here, keeping everything before it frictionless.

Verification Gate

A reusable component across all three tiers, so users learn one pattern regardless of what they are verifying. Tier 2 triggers at the point of publishing, not at the point of creating a listing. That lets owners write and edit before they are asked to verify.

Identity Verification

Capture, failure, processing, and success are states within a single flow, not separate screens. High-value listings use the same gate with escalated prompts and checks for Tier 3. There is no separate review page because the gate handles the escalation itself.

Owner Trust Profile

A transparent trust panel visible directly on the Item Details sidebar. This consolidates identity verification status, historical rental metrics, and earned behavioral badges into a single, explainable Trust Score. Users can expand the score details to inspect the direct contributing factors, ensuring the platform's trust evaluation remains completely open and non-opaque.

Considered, Not Built

Decisions by omission.

A few directions were scoped out, then either rejected or deferred. Each one had a real reason.

Rent price calculator / availability filter

Addresses pricing and discovery, not verification or trust. Out of scope for this build.

Dual renter/lister hero storytelling

Deferred. The hero handles this through a single line of copy rather than splitting the interface into two entry points.

Optional fields during verification

Portfolio links, social handles, and purpose-to-rent fields were rejected. Collecting non-required data during an already friction-heavy step makes drop-off worse without improving trust. It also runs against the minimal-necessary-verification-per-tier principle in Section 2.

Use-case based browse filters

A promising direction for discovery. Deferred for this timeline.

Validation

Measuring whether the trade-off actually holds.

Engagement Metrics
Verification Gate Conversion Rate
The percentage of users who click continue when the gate opens rather than closing the window, measuring early friction.
Verification Completion Rate by Tier
Tracks how many users finish verification once started. This helps check if their buying intent is strong enough to handle the extra steps.
Outcome Metrics
Transaction Completion After Verification
Measures how many users return to complete their booking after verifying, which indicates overall onboarding success.
Listing Publish Completion Rate
The percentage of owners who finish their ID and address checks after writing their listing, monitoring seller drop-off.
System Performance
Time to Verified Status
Tracks the time from starting the check to receiving a trust score, ensuring the background review process doesn't hold up the rental.
First-Attempt Capture Success Rate
The share of photo captures that pass automated quality checks on the first attempt, showing if the camera UI is clear.
Selfie/Liveness Retry Rate
How often users have to retake their selfie due to failed checks, indicating camera or hardware issues.
Manual Review Escalation Rate
The rate of verifications sent to human reviewers, which highlights how often the automated check fails.

Every screen answers a decision, not a checklist item.

Tying verification to transaction risk keeps the marketplace open for anyone who wants to browse. Trust is not earned once at sign-up. It scales with what a user actually does.
